Pets change the herb plan. The goal is not a sterile home — it is **no chewed alliums, no tipped nutrient water, and no perfume cloud in a studio**.

![Grocery herbs and greens — start with what you cook, keep pots elevated](/images/inline-grocery-herbs.jpg)

**Disclaimer:** Toxicity varies by animal, amount, and plant part. Confirm specific plants with a vet or a trusted toxicity database (e.g. ASPCA) before leaving greenery where pets graze. This guide is rental/apartment setup advice, not medical guidance.

## Quick picks

| Goal | Grow | Skip or isolate |
|------|------|-----------------|
| Cats/dogs that chew plants | Parsley, basil (out of reach) | Chives, garlic greens, onion family |
| Low smell in a studio | Parsley, young basil in small pots | Big mint thickets, heavy rosemary |
| Covered / harder to tip | Click & Grow or kit with stable base | Tall top-heavy towers on skinny stands |
| DIY under $50 | [Under-$50 Sill Kit](/kits/under-50/) on a high sill | Floor-level jar farms |

## Decision rules

1. **Assume curious pets will taste leaves** — elevate or gate pots.
2. **Allium family is the hard no for chewers** — chives look innocent and are a common mistake.
3. **Nutrient water is not a pet bowl** — cover reservoirs; wipe spills.
4. **Smell is dose-dependent** — three mint pots beside the bed will perfume the room; one parsley pot will not.
5. **Kit pods reduce soil digging** but do not make a plant “pet proof.”

## Treat with extra caution

- **Chives / garlic greens / onion family** — commonly listed as toxic to dogs and cats. Skip if pets graze.
- **Large mint patches** — usually not the toxicity headline, but mint can upset stomachs in quantity and the smell fills a studio. One pot on a high sill is enough.
- **Essential-oil “pet safe” sprays** — unrelated to growing food herbs; do not improvise.

## Low-smell studio strategy

| Do | Don’t |
|----|-------|
| One mild herb at a time near the bed | A mint hedge on the nightstand |
| Harvest often (less blooming scent) | Let herbs flower and perfume the room |
| Use a light hood / schedule for kits | 16-hour LED blast beside a pillow |
| Prefer parsley + basil rotation | Rosemary + lavender “spa shelf” in 200 sq ft |

## Placement and hardware

- High sill or sturdy cart pets cannot launch onto.
- Boot tray under everything — see [landlord-safe checklist](/guides/landlord-safe-indoor-garden-setup/).
- Avoid open Kratky jars at cat nose height; if you DIY, use [Homemade Kratky Kit](/kits/homemade-kratky/) on an elevated tray and supervise.
- Clip lights with secure clamps — chewing cords is a separate hazard (use cord covers / out-of-reach routing).

## If a pet eats a plant

Contact a vet or poison hotline with the plant name. Do not wait on internet anecdotes. Remove remaining accessible greenery.

> **Key takeaway**  
> Elevate mild culinary herbs, skip alliums if pets chew, and keep nutrient water covered. Low smell means smaller amounts — not a special “odorless” product.

## FAQ

**Can I grow herbs with a cat?**  
Many people do — with elevated pots and no chives/garlic greens in chew range. Confirm plants for your situation.

**What is the least smelly apartment herb?**  
Parsley is a practical mild default. Smell also drops when you grow less plant mass.

**Are hydroponic nutrients safe if a dog drinks the reservoir?**  
Do not let that happen. Cover reservoirs and clean spills; call a vet if ingestion occurs.
