Landlords rarely ban “herbs.” They care about water damage, mold, and holes in walls. Build your garden around those three.

![Oregano seedlings in a labeled cup (with tomato starters nearby)](/images/inline-oregano.jpg)

## Checklist

- [ ] **Boot tray or waterproof mat** under every reservoir and pot  
- [ ] **No ceiling hooks / no drilled grow-light mounts**  
- [ ] **Freestanding only** — countertop kit or shelf you already own  
- [ ] **Wipe condensation** on cold windows in winter  
- [ ] **Don’t overfill reservoirs** before travel days  
- [ ] **Power strip with surge protection** — not a daisy chain of cheap adapters  
- [ ] **Pet plan** — covered reservoirs or placement pets can’t tip  
- [ ] **Move test** — you can carry the whole setup to the sink in one trip  
- [ ] **Exit plan** — nothing glued, screwed, or painted onto landlord surfaces  

## Decision rules

1. **Water damage > plant death** — a dead basil plant is cheaper than a ruined windowsill.
2. **Freestanding beats “pro” mounts** — tension rods and adhesive hooks still fail; skip them.
3. **One continuous tray** beats a constellation of saucers that overflow into each other.
4. **Humidity is a winter problem** — cold glass + warm moist air = drip lines landlords notice.
5. **If the lease forbids water beds / aquariums**, treat large open reservoirs with the same caution — keep volume small and contained.

## Humidity note

A few jars of basil won’t steam the apartment. A crowded tower plus poor airflow can. If walls feel damp near the unit, reduce plant count or add a small fan on low. Wipe the window ledge weekly in heating season.

## Placement that protects the deposit

| Do | Don’t |
|----|-------|
| Countertop / desk with boot tray | Bare wood furniture without a tray |
| Clip LED on a shelf you own | Ceiling hooks or drilled brackets |
| Kratky jars on one tray | Jars directly on painted sills |
| Surge-protected strip | Daisy-chained USB bricks on the floor |
| Covered or elevated reservoirs with pets | Open nutrient water at cat nose height |

## Move-out day

1. Empty reservoirs into the sink (not onto the sill).  
2. Dry trays completely.  
3. Wipe mineral spots on counters.  
4. Unplug timers/strips; leave no adhesive residue.  
5. Photograph the clean sill if you want deposit proof.

> **Key takeaway**  
> Trays + freestanding gear + no drilling covers almost every deposit risk. Buy only what you can pack out in five minutes.

## FAQ

**Will a countertop garden void my lease?**  
Usually no — freestanding plants are normal. Water stains and drilled mounts are what trigger deposit fights.

**Do I need a waterproof tray for a sealed kit?**  
Yes. Kits still get topped up, tipped, or condensated. Trays are cheap insurance.

**Is Kratky landlord-safe?**  
Yes if jars sit on a waterproof tray and you do not overfill before travel. See [Kratky guide](/guides/kratky-jar-herbs-apartment/).
