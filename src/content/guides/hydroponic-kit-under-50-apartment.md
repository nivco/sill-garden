## Why Kratky is the under-$50 hydroponic kit

Kratky is a still-water method: roots sit in nutrient solution, an air gap stays above the water line, and there is **no pump**. That is why it fits apartments that need silence and a small budget.

You need:

1. A jar you can see through (so you notice the water line).
2. A net pot and a medium that holds the stem.
3. Hydroponic nutrient mixed to the bottle’s herb rate — not “a splash of plant food.”
4. A tray under the jar.
5. Light: a bright window **or** a clip LED a few inches above the leaves.

It loses when you travel for two weeks, when the jar sits in a dark kitchen, or when you wanted a gadget with a timer. Those wants cost more than $50.

## Decision rules

1. **Count the light.** No lamp in the box and a dim counter means the real price is kit + LED.
2. **Count the pump.** Under $50 and silent means Kratky or soil, not a waterfall tower.
3. **Count pods you will eat.** One jar of basil you cook beats twelve pods of garnish.
4. **Cross $50 on purpose.** Upgrade after one Kratky or soil harvest, not because a carousel said “best hydroponic kit.”

## FAQ

**Best hydroponic kit under 50?**  
A Kratky jar kit: jar, net pot, nutrient, and a tray. Add a clip light if the spot fails a sharp-shadow test at noon. Branded pod gardens are the next step up, not this price.

**Is there a silent hydroponic garden under 50?**  
Yes — Kratky has no pump. Wick gardens such as Click & Grow are also silent, but they usually cost more than $50.

**Can I grow basil hydroponically in an apartment for under 50?**  
Yes, if the jar gets enough light. Basil in a dark corner dies at any price. Mint and chives are more forgiving while you learn the water line.

**Should I buy a 12-pod kit because it is on sale under 50?**  
Only if the listing includes a real grow light and you have counter depth and tolerance for pump noise. Most “12 pods under $50” listings fail one of those checks.
